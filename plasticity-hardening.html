Isotropic, Kinematic or Combined Hardening?

A practical guide to understanding how hardening models influence yielding, reverse loading, cyclic plasticity and finite element predictions.

Metal Plasticity

Why does the hardening model matter?

When a metal plastically deforms, its subsequent yielding behaviour depends on how the yield condition evolves.

In finite element analysis, this behaviour is commonly represented using isotropic hardening, kinematic hardening, or a combination of both.

The difference becomes especially important when the loading path changes direction, such as during unloading, reverse loading, cyclic deformation, bending and springback.
