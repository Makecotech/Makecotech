Smoothing methods — theory & guidance

Local quadratic regression

A quadratic is fitted repeatedly around each evaluation position. The before- and after-deposition profiles are fitted separately, then their local curvature difference is used for Stoney-equivalent stress.

z ≈ a + b·(x−x₀) + c·(x−x₀)² → κ = 2c / (1+b²)^(3/2)

Smaller window: preserves local S/M-shape features but follows measurement noise more strongly. Larger window: gives smoother curvature/stress but can smear localized features.

Best for: non-uniform bow, asymmetric profiles, local curvature changes, and experimental scans where spatial variation matters.

Global quadratic

One quadratic is fitted to the entire retained diameter after edge exclusion. It gives a single classical, nearly constant-curvature representation and is naturally less sensitive to point-to-point noise.

one quadratic fit over the full retained profile → one global κ

Local S-shapes, M-shapes and edge/local variations are intentionally averaged out.

Best for: classical Stoney analysis, approximately parabolic wafers, benchmark mean curvature, or a stable global stress value.

Important: the measured before/after Z profile is never replaced by either fit. The selected method changes only curvature and derived stress. Curvature is a second derivative, so smoothing can strongly affect local peaks; compare more than one reasonable local window when peak stress matters.

Expected behavior: changing Local ↔ Global must not change the measured before/after Z profile or 3D wafer geometry. Only curvature and Stoney-equivalent stress should change.

Measurement / processing

Stress averaging — formulas & interpretation

Every reported stress value starts from the local Stoney-equivalent relation:

σᵢ = [Mₛ hₛ² / (6 t_f)] · Δκᵢ

Single 1D diameter scan

The reported mean is the arithmetic mean of the valid local stress values along the retained scan:

σ̄_scan = (1/N) · Σ σᵢ

This is a line average, not a whole-wafer area average.

Two orthogonal scans (0° + 90°)

Each direction is averaged separately, then both scan directions are given equal weight:

σ̄_dir = ½(σ̄₀° + σ̄₉₀°)

The summary also shows the 0° and 90° means individually so directional anisotropy remains visible. This is still not a full-area integral.

Full x,y,z wafer map

Stress is calculated from local mean-curvature change ΔH on each valid, equally spaced internal grid cell. The reported area mean is:

σ̄_area ≈ [Σ σᵢⱼ ΔA] / [Σ ΔA] = (1/N_valid) · Σ σᵢⱼ

Because the internal x,y grid has uniform cell area, equal-cell averaging is equivalent to area weighting over the valid measured region after edge exclusion.

Full x,y,z curvature fitting

Full-field mode does not use the 1D Local/Global selector. At each evaluation location, a local quadratic surface is fitted directly to nearby measured x,y,z points; derivatives are obtained analytically rather than by differentiating an interpolated contour.

z ≈ a + bx + cy + dx² + exy + fy² → κx, κy, κ1, κ2, H

Interpretation: scan means, directional averages and area means answer different questions. The summary label changes automatically so a 1D line average is never presented as a whole-wafer average.

Physics, sign convention & references

Physics / sign convention

  • +Z is upward. Positive curvature is concave-up / bow-down.
  • Positive Δκ or ΔH maps to tensile Stoney-equivalent film stress; negative maps to compressive.
  • Local 1D mode uses quadratic fitting; full x,y,z mode computes surface curvatures from spatial derivatives.
  • Edge exclusion removes edge points from curvature fitting; no-data zones are not treated as zero stress.
